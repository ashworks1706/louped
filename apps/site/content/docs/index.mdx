loupe is a local testbed for research on language models along two axes: **behavior and
alignment** (what models do and why) and **efficiency and systems** (what it costs to run them).
Each research question is an experiment, filed under a domain, with a stated hypothesis, a
baseline, a test and a result you can reproduce and read in one app.

Every experiment uses the same method. Change the model: steer or ablate a direction, fine-tune it,
rewrite its prompt, add an adapter, swap its attention kernel. Measure what changed: the same evals
run on both versions, sample by sample, with paired intervals and a verdict on what moved and what
held. Explain it: logit lens, patching, probes, SAE features and circuits on the same model under
the same change.

A change is one spec, a policy, that runs the same way in an eval, a training run, the Playground
and an analysis. Your question is a short script; loupe supplies the rest and the app to launch and
read it from. It runs on your machine or your cluster: open-weight models, results in your
project's `.loupe/`, no hosted model and no API key.

## How it is used

1. **Make a project**: `pip install 'loupelab[server,tracking,interp,agent]'`, then
   `loupe init my-research` and `loupe serve`. The project holds your questions under
   `experiments/`, an example that runs on a CPU, and what your coding agent needs.
2. **Ask**: tell your agent (Claude Code, Codex, Cursor) what to test. Through loupe's MCP server it
   starts the experiment and writes the README (question, hypotheses, baseline, test, stop if)
   before the code.
3. **Run**: a small check here, live in the app; a real run exported to Sol, a Slurm cluster or a
   VM and imported back. A model loupe does not load is evaluated and timed through its endpoint.
4. **Read**: on the run's page, every item under every condition against a fixed cohort, the
   hardware and energy it used, its provenance, and its files (reports, records, traces as
   timelines). The agent reads the same results and reports them with their denominators.

Results made without loupe open read-only with `loupe view <folder>`.
